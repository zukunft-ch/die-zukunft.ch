Since 1 March 2025, Switzerland has had a legal basis for automated driving: a motorway pilot, driverless vehicles on routes approved by a canton, and automated parking. According to the Federal Roads Office (FEDRO/ASTRA), however, not a single automated vehicle has yet been approved, and for private cars the law still thinks in terms of drivers. We propose two steps: cantons designate operating areas, and the Confederation makes clear that occupants of a vehicle that drives itself and requires no takeover do not need a driving licence.

---

## Methodology

This note summarises the legal situation under the Road Traffic Act (SVG, Title IIa, Art. 25a ff.) and the Ordinance on Automated Driving (VAF), based on FEDRO's pages, the ordinance text and a legal commentary. It contains no measurement data of its own. Status of pilot projects as per FEDRO's overview of June 2026. The proposals at the end are the party's political demands, not legal advice.

---

### What applies today

- **Motorway pilot:** when an approved system is active, drivers may take their hands off the wheel on Swiss motorways but must be able to take over at any time. Whoever drives this way remains the driver and needs a licence. Since 1 July 2025, such systems have been part of the driving test.
- **Driverless vehicles:** they may only run on routes approved by a canton. An operator supervises them from a control centre in Switzerland, may look after several vehicles at once and needs at least a category B licence. FEDRO's directives on approving operating areas have applied since 1 January 2026.
- **Automated parking:** in designated, signposted car parks the vehicle may park with nobody at the wheel.

### What is missing

- **Vehicles:** according to FEDRO, no automated vehicle has yet been approved for Swiss roads. Switzerland recognises EU and UNECE type approvals, so the pace depends heavily on Europe.
- **Routes:** there is no public list of approved operating areas yet. What runs, runs as a pilot under an exceptional permit (Art. 25h SVG), such as the Level 4 mobility service "iamo" in the Furttal (canton of Zurich) or earlier shuttle trials in Sion, Schaffhausen and Geneva. Swiss Post is planning highly automated vehicles in eastern Switzerland.
- **Private vehicles:** today only a supervised vehicle on an approved route may run driverless, in practice a fleet with a control centre. A private car that drives itself within an approved area is not provided for; whoever sits in it counts as the driver as soon as they operate it.

### Who benefits

The gain lies less in convenience than with people who are not mobile today: older people who have had to give up their licence, people with visual or physical impairments, young people in villages without a late connection, everyone who never learned to drive. Add off-peak hours and feeder routes in public transport, where a bus with a driver is too expensive.

### International

- **Vienna Convention:** the 1968 Convention on Road Traffic (SR 0.741.10), to which Switzerland is party, required a driver for every vehicle. The new Article 34bis, in force since 14 July 2022, deems this requirement met while an automated driving system complying with the respective country's rules is driving. International law therefore does not stand in the way of dropping the licence requirement.
- **Germany:** since 2021, German road traffic law (§§ 1d–1l StVG) has allowed Level 4 vehicles without a driver in defined operating areas, with technical supervision outside the vehicle. According to the Bundestag, Level 4 has not yet been introduced on a significant scale there either.

---

## What this means for policy

**If you don't drive, you don't need a licence.** Whoever drives still needs a licence. Only when the system fully takes over the driving task within an approved area and requires no takeover are the occupants passengers, as on a bus or a train.

- **Cantons:** designate operating areas, first where public transport has gaps (off-peak hours, villages, feeders to the station), with a timetable and a public list. This can be done with cantonal instruments, such as an individual or popular initiative in the canton of Zurich.
- **Confederation:** amend the SVG so that occupants of such vehicles need no licence and private vehicles may also drive automatically within approved areas. This requires a parliamentary motion (draft below).
- **Liability:** injured parties are already protected by the keeper's strict liability (Art. 58 SVG) and compulsory liability insurance. What needs to be settled in law is recourse against manufacturers and operators when the system was driving.
- **Measure:** publish approved operating areas, kilometres driven and incidents every year: [Robotics](@/robotik.en.md).

## Draft: motion

**Automated driving without a driving licence. If you don't drive, you are a passenger**

The Federal Council is instructed to submit to Parliament an amendment to the Road Traffic Act so that:

1. persons sitting in a vehicle whose automated system fully takes over the driving task within an approved operating area and requires no takeover are not considered drivers and do not need a driving licence;
2. privately owned vehicles with such systems may also be operated within cantonally approved operating areas, with supervision proportionate to the risk;
3. recourse of the liability insurer against manufacturers and operators for damage caused in automated operation is regulated;
4. FEDRO keeps a public register of approved operating areas, pilot projects and reported incidents.

*Rationale:* since 1 March 2025, the SVG has allowed driverless vehicles on approved routes. Since 2022, the Vienna Convention has allowed an automated driving system to satisfy the driver requirement. What remains open is who may sit in the vehicle. If you don't drive, you should not need a licence. This opens up mobility for older people, people with disabilities and young people in rural areas, without lowering the safety requirements for the vehicles.
