Singapore (ONE Pass), Hong Kong (Top Talent Pass Scheme) and the United Kingdom (Global Talent) have created dedicated admission routes for top talent. What they have in common: clear criteria instead of a case-by-case labour market test, a decision within around four to eight weeks, and family included. Switzerland has no such route; researchers and founders from third countries go through the same quota procedure as all qualified workers. The point here is a narrow, fast route for a few thousand people a year, not more general immigration.

<!-- more -->

---

## Methodology

We compare criteria, processing times, family reunification and case numbers, in each case from the information published by the competent authorities: Ministry of Manpower (Singapore), Immigration Department and Labour and Welfare Bureau (Hong Kong), Home Office (United Kingdom), State Secretariat for Migration and Federal Council (Switzerland). The case numbers are not directly comparable: the Swiss quotas apply to all qualified workers from third countries, not only to top talent; for Hong Kong only cumulative figures are available. Two values are therefore marked as estimates.

---

### Criteria and family

- **Singapore, ONE Pass (since 2023):** fixed monthly salary of at least 30,000 Singapore dollars with an established company, or outstanding achievements in research, science, culture or sport. Valid for up to 5 years; changing jobs, working for several employers or running one's own company without a new application. Spouses can work with a Letter of Consent.
- **Hong Kong, Top Talent Pass (since end of 2022):** annual income of at least HK$2.5 million or a degree from a university on the official list of top universities (with or without work experience). No job offer required. Stay of 24 to 36 months, extension only with employment or one's own company. Spouses are allowed to work.
- **United Kingdom, Global Talent (since 2020):** endorsement by a specialist body in research, culture or digital technology, or a recognised prize. No job offer required. Up to 5 years per grant, settlement possible after 3 or 5 years; partners and children can accompany.
- **Switzerland, admission from third countries:** application by the employer, proof of priority for domestic workers, local and industry-standard pay and working conditions, qualification as a manager, specialist or qualified worker (Art. 20–23 AIG [Foreign Nationals and Integration Act]). For 2025 and 2026, 8,500 quota permits apply in each year (4,500 B, 4,000 L). Family reunification for B permit holders is subject to conditions (Art. 44 AIG).

### Processing time

{% chart(spec="talent_bearbeitungszeit", alt="Official guideline processing times for talent visas in weeks") %}
Singapore states "within 4 weeks for most cases", Hong Kong "normally around 4 weeks" after a complete application. In the United Kingdom, endorsement takes 2 weeks (with a job, fellowship or funding) to 5 weeks (peer review), followed by the visa decision in around 3 weeks. In Switzerland, the SEM expects 6 to 8 weeks "as a rule" from a complete application, longer for complex cases. A target of ten working days would therefore be faster than all three models; it presupposes a simplified, standardised procedure.
{% end %}

### Case numbers

{% chart(spec="talent_erteilungen", alt="Permits per year for talent programmes and Swiss third-country quotas") %}
Singapore issued around 3,000 ONE Passes in 2024; at the end of 2025 there were around 8,500 holders. The United Kingdom granted 3,877 Global Talent visas to main applicants outside the country in 2024. Hong Kong had approved over 120,000 applications by the end of January 2026, on average (estimate) just over 38,000 per year. Switzerland used 74% of its third-country quotas in 2024 (estimated at around 6,300), and around 52% by the end of September 2025. The quotas are therefore not the bottleneck; for top talent it is rather the procedures and their duration.
{% end %}

---

### United Kingdom: stable demand, few refusals

{% chart(spec="talent_uk_verlauf", alt="UK Global Talent: visas granted to main applicants and dependants, 2019 to 2025") %}
The number of Global Talent visas (in 2019 and partly 2020 still its predecessor, Exceptional Talent) rose from 794 (2019) to 4,112 (2023) and has since been around 3,900 per year; in addition, around 2,800 dependants each year. Refusals at the visa stage are rare (2025: 26), because selection takes place at the endorsement stage. Hong Kong shows the other side: of around 40,200 people whose first visa expired by January 2026, only around 20,800 applied for an extension. Broad criteria generate many applications, but not automatically people who stay.
{% end %}

## What this means for policy

- **A dedicated talent visa outside the quotas:** narrow, verifiable criteria (salary threshold or endorsement by the ETH Domain, universities, SNSF, Innosuisse), a decision within ten working days, family included: [Talent, Integration & Rule of Law](@/talent-integration.md).
- **Start small and measurable:** an order of magnitude like the United Kingdom or Singapore (a few thousand per year), not like Hong Kong; an annual report on permits granted, retention and company formations.
- **Measure retention, not volume:** the Hong Kong example shows that extension rates are more meaningful than application numbers.
