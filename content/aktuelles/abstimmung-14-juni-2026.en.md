On 14 June 2026 Switzerland votes on two federal proposals: the popular initiative "No to a Switzerland with 10 million! (Sustainability Initiative)" and the amendment to the Civilian Service Act. Both touch questions we have worked on from the start — how to attract talent, how to keep cities and infrastructure sustainable, how to build civil and military readiness at the same time. We do not issue a voting recommendation here. We show where the two proposals meet our program, and where, from our perspective, the honest tensions lie.

**Update (result).** Both proposals have been decided: the popular initiative "No to a Switzerland with 10 million!" was rejected with 54.8% no, and the amendment to the Civilian Service Act was accepted with 53.0% yes (turnout 58.9%). The framing below was written before voting Sunday and stands unchanged as a program reading.

---

## Proposal 1: "No to a Switzerland with 10 million!"

The SVP initiative wants to cap Switzerland's permanent resident population at ten million people by 2050. If that threshold were crossed earlier, the Federal Council would have to renegotiate or terminate the free movement of persons agreements with the EU. The Federal Council and Parliament recommend rejection.

Our program reads this proposal along two pillars.

**Talent & integration.** Switzerland owes its prosperity to its ability to attract researchers, engineers, care workers and founders. A rigid cap is a blunt tool: it does not distinguish between unmanaged growth and the targeted inflow of specialists who will carry the next twenty years. Talent pathways need to be shorter, not longer.

**Cities & housing.** The other half of the truth: infrastructure — housing, public transport, schools, hospitals, energy — has for years grown more slowly than the population. Whoever wants inflow must also build, plan, train. The initiative responds to a real failure, but with a quantity cap rather than with construction and planning capacity.

The question behind the proposal is therefore not "how many people" but "how much substance do we build so that Switzerland can carry the people it needs". A ceiling without a plan solves none of the underlying problems.

---

## Proposal 2: amendment to the Civilian Service Act

Parliament revised the Civilian Service Act, a referendum brought the proposal before the people. Six measures are intended to make admission to civilian service harder, so that the constitutional rule — no free choice between army and civilian service — is enforced more strictly. The Federal Council and Parliament recommend acceptance.

Our program reads this proposal along one pillar.

**Neutrality & peace.** Switzerland needs both: a credible army and a civil resilience that absorbs crises — blackouts, pandemics, cyberattacks, climate events. People in civilian service work in hospitals, in agriculture, in nature conservation, in schools. They are not a residual position but part of a country's civil readiness.

The tension in this proposal is real and honest: every person who switches to civilian service is missing from the army. But every person who cannot enter civilian service is missing from civil supply. The question is whether Switzerland should solve a shortage in one place by creating a shortage in another — or whether both pillars need to be strengthened at the same time.

By the logic of our program, this is not a pure army question. It is a question of how we understand resilience as a whole.

---

## How we frame it

Both proposals address real Swiss problems — infrastructure pressure and security architecture — with tools we consider too narrow. A population cap is not a housing policy. An admission hurdle is not a civil protection strategy.

Our contribution is not to tell the population where to put the cross. Our contribution is to describe the long-term substance against which Switzerland will be measured over the next twenty years. We have summarized it in our [program](@/programm.en.md) as twelve pillars. The two proposals on 14 June touch three of them. Whoever votes can take this reading as one voice among many.
