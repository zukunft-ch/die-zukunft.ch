1 August commemorates a federal charter from 1291. 735 years on, we are not celebrating a museum piece but an idea that has stayed remarkably modern: that people shape their future better together and self-determined than alone and governed from outside.

---

## A founding act that faced forward

1291 was not a look backward. It was a decision to reorder how people lived together, to create legal certainty, and to withstand outside pressure jointly. Switzerland was not born of nostalgia but of a pragmatic need for stability and self-determination.

It is precisely that instinct — solving problems soberly, building institutions that last, choosing reliability over loud gestures — that has carried the country through seven centuries. It is not an heirloom to put on a shelf. It is a way of working.

---

## The substance is built today

The coming decades bring shifts that reach deeper than any single term of office: artificial intelligence, energy, demographics, the new order of the world economy. The question is not whether Switzerland will change. The question is whether it shapes that change itself — sovereign, resilient, clear-eyed — or lets it happen to it.

What we build or neglect over the next ten years will decide how free and prosperous Switzerland is in 2050. That substance appears in no ranking. It is being built now.

---

## A holiday is also a charge

On 1 August we rightly celebrate what the generations before us built: a country that works, that holds its diversity together, that enjoys trust beyond its size. To preserve that inheritance is not to freeze it. It is to keep building it.

The best way to honor Switzerland is to keep it fit for the future.
